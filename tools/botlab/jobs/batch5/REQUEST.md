The shots that it can fire range from a small twister or burst to a large tornado-like twister or big, explosive burst depending on the length of time that is held down by the player. At maximum charge, the shot is lethal, allowing the Rainmaker carrier to have a form of defense against enemy fire. The projectile is fired in an arc, allowing its shots to hit enemies behind cover but making it much less effective in close quarters.
The Rainmaker carrier should note that shots from the Rainmaker do not consume ink, and therefore they do not need to consider refilling their [ink tank](https://splatoonwiki.org/wiki/Ink_tank) when defending or attacking.

* The carrier on the losing team gets splatted while holding the Rainmaker.

# Designing Splatoon's Stages Around Rainmaker (Splatoon, Splatoon 2, Splatoon 3)

Nintendo's patch notes show that across all three games, stages kept being changed to fit Rainmaker. Over and over, the fix was to add space and routes (deeper spawns, more ways into the center, goals moved further back). Two other tools were used alongside it: Rainmaker-specific layout variants and "Rainmaker-Free Zones." Some stage/Rainmaker pairings were simply pulled from rotation until they were fixed.

## TL;DR
- **Why Rainmaker is hard on maps:** the carrier walks from the center to a goal in the enemy base. They move 20% slower, can't Super Jump, can't use their own weapon, and are marked for everyone. They get a lethal charge shot and a 60-second explosion timer. That makes knockout distance, flank routes, spawn depth and goal placement matter more than in any other mode. Nintendo has given every stage its own Rainmaker layout since Splatoon (2015).
- **Confirmed redesigns:**
  - Splatoon: Ver. 2.2.0 changed Flounder Heights' Rainmaker layout; Ver. 2.4.0 added Rainmaker "prohibited areas."
  - Splatoon 2: Starfish Mainstage (4.0.0), The Reef (4.2.0) and Blackbelly Skatepark (4.3.0) were deepened and widened. Blackbelly's Rainmaker layout had been absent from rotation for about a year before 4.3.0.
  - Splatoon 3: Mahi-Mahi Resort (5.1.0), Undertow Spillway (7.2.0), and Hammerhead Bridge and Barnacle & Dime (8.0.0, "to increase routes into the center and options for strategic points") were reworked.
- **What's confirmed and what isn't:** patch notes say *what* changed, almost never *why*. I found no primary developer quote about designing stages specifically for Rainmaker. Claims that a given rework was "for Rainmaker" or "against spawn camping" are mostly wiki, press or community readings of Nintendo's blueprints.

## Key Findings

### 1. How Rainmaker works and why it strains stage layouts
Mechanics, from Inkipedia's mode page:
- **Getting and carrying it:** the Rainmaker starts at the center inside a shield. Breaking the shield splats nearby enemies, and touching it hurts.
- **Carrier limits:** the carrier can't use their own main, sub or special weapons and can't Super Jump or enter a spawn point. Movement and swim speed drop by 20%, and their special gauge stops charging.
- **Visibility:** a marker shows the carrier to everyone, even through walls when in humanoid form.
- **Timer:** it explodes after 60 seconds, splatting the carrier and nearby teammates.
- **Weapon change between games:** Splatoon's version fired tornado twisters. From Splatoon 2 on, it fires an arcing, exploding ink ball that can hit behind cover but is weak up close.
- **Penalties:** retreating behind the team's best progress shows "Don't retreat!" and makes the timer run three times faster. In Splatoon 2 and 3, if the carrier blows up on their own side, the Rainmaker resets to the center.
- **Splatoon 3 checkpoints:** one or two checkpoints sit between the center and the goal. A team must plant the Rainmaker on a checkpoint before it can score any progress beyond it. Planting lowers the checkpoints, raises the goal and re-forms the shield.

What this means for stage design (my analysis based on these mechanics):
- **Distance to the goal matters most.** A slow, visible carrier with a timer needs a path long enough that one good push doesn't win in seconds, but short enough that a push can realistically succeed. Blackbelly Skatepark is the textbook failure: Inkipedia says its old layout "allowed Rainmaker carriers to easily score a knockout within the first minute of a match."
- **Flank routes and chokepoints.** The carrier can't jump and fights with a slow arcing shot, so a single corridor makes a good defense unbeatable. Too many hidden routes do the opposite and let a carrier slip in. That is why so many patches close "shortcuts" or unintended ways into the enemy base in Rainmaker specifically.
- **Spawn depth and the "safe" back area.** The defending team needs room to re-form, and the attacker must not be able to hide the Rainmaker on unreachable high ground. Nintendo's answer was Rainmaker-Free Zones (prohibited areas), added in Splatoon Ver. 2.4.0 (17 Dec 2015). Entering your own side's zone with the Rainmaker speeds up the timer. If it drops there, it respawns at the center.
- **Where the zones are:**
  - Splatoon: Walleye Warehouse, Saltspray Rig, Arowana Mall and Flounder Heights (added in 2.4.0), plus Piranha Pit.
  - Splatoon 2: 17 stages, including The Reef, Port Mackerel, Blackbelly Skatepark, MakoMart, Shellendorf Institute, Arowana Mall, Piranha Pit and Wahoo World.
  - Splatoon 3: a large share of the roster, including Scorch Gorge, Eeltail Alley, Hagglefish Market, Undertow Spillway, Hammerhead Bridge, Museum d'Alfonsino, Mahi-Mahi Resort, Sturgeon Shipyard, Brinewater Springs, Flounder Heights, Um'ami Ruins, Robo ROM-en, Marlin Airport and Urchin Underpass.
- **Goal pedestal placement.** Nintendo repeatedly moved goals further back or down a level, for example at Starfish Mainstage in 4.0.0 and The Reef in 4.2.0.

### 2. Every stage has a Rainmaker-specific layout
Nintendo states that stages have "slight layout differences between modes." Inkipedia's stage pages describe each Ranked layout as changing "the terrain or placement of objects… these changes create new paths or new objects to take cover behind." Notable Rainmaker examples:

| Stage (game) | Rainmaker-specific layout features |
|---|---|
| Blackbelly Skatepark (S1) | Rainmaker on top of the central tower; goals just off the spawn. Black uninkable boxes near the goals. Small boxes added to the tower so players can hit the shield without being pushed off. |
| Blackbelly Skatepark (S2) | Central tower much shorter with the Rainmaker on it. Goal a layer below spawn, with a block added to drop to it. The only Ranked mode on this stage that keeps most of the Turf War terrain. |
| Hammerhead Bridge (S1) | Rainmaker at the center on top of the grated bridge; goals in front of each spawn. "Many stacks of inkable crates" added to create attack routes into bases. |
| Hammerhead Bridge (S3) | Starts on a center block; one checkpoint right next to the start; goal deep in the enemy spawn. Small boxes and Bumpers added for cover, plus a jump block at center-right. |
| Mahi-Mahi Resort (S1) | Starts on a large crate. Extra floating platforms and boxes, and a row of balloons extended to block Rainmaker shots. Water drops once a team's count reaches 40, revealing platforms, walls and a ramp into the enemy base. |
| Mahi-Mahi Resort (S3) | Center "changed drastically": raised center islands removed and ramps added toward the enemy's raised area. Checkpoints in that raised area and at the base entrance; pedestal near the back of the base. Water drops after a checkpoint is cleared. |
| Starfish Mainstage (S2) | Inkrails added; a ramp into the lower plaza; a glass structure removed. Extra turf and grates to the right of spawn. |
| Port Mackerel (S1/S2) | Goal is a pillar on top of the cargo container in front of the enemy spawn, reached by climbing a board placed for this mode. |
| Moray Towers (S1) | Starts on the small central incline; goal below the first big drop outside the enemy base. |
| The Reef (S2, pre-4.2.0) | Starts below the bridge; goals by the parked cars, with ramps added into each base. |
| Eeltail Alley (S3) | Checkpoints in front of the bridge and in the middle of each team's side; goal on the first drop from spawn. |

### 3. Confirmed stage changes for Rainmaker, by game and version

**Splatoon (Wii U)**
- **Ver. 2.0.0 (5 Aug 2015):** Urchin Underpass got a near-total rebuild just before Rainmaker launched on 15 Aug 2015. It was announced in an in-character "SAFETY NOTICE" on the Squid Research Lab Tumblr (27 Jul 2015), with no design reason given.
  - Changes (per Inkipedia): alleys lengthened and widened; center ramps halved; trees moved.
  - It was the first "closed for renovations" event, and later Splatoon 2 renovations were explicitly compared to it.
  - The link to Rainmaker is only timing; nothing official ties the two.
- **Ver. 2.2.0 (21 Oct 2015), official notes:**
  - Walleye Warehouse: "prevent users from taking certain shortcuts in Rainmaker mode."
  - Kelp Dome: "Small adjustments will be made for Rainmaker mode."
  - Flounder Heights: "In addition to changing the stage layout in Rainmaker, other adjustments…"
  - Fixed "the Rainmaker shield does not take damage when the shield is extremely close to a wall."
  - Increased Slosher and Tri-Slosher damage to Rainmaker shields by about 33%.
  - Also in 2.2.0: a Hammerhead Bridge exploit where dropping onto support beams while holding the Rainmaker made it reappear there was patched.
- **Ver. 2.4.0 (17 Dec 2015):** added the prohibited areas. Museum d'Alfonsino: "prevent users from taking certain shortcuts in Rainmaker mode."
- **Ver. 2.5.0 (19 Jan 2016):** Walleye Warehouse "object placement in Rainmaker mode."
- **Ver. 2.8.0 (8 Jun 2016):** Camp Triggerfish's "Don't retreat!" zone near the team starting areas was resized.
- **Pairings removed from rotation:** rotation data from Aug 2015 to Dec 2016 shows some stages simply didn't appear in Rainmaker for long periods. This looks like Nintendo removing pairings rather than reworking them.
  - Saltspray Rig: Oct–Nov 2015, then Feb–Dec 2016
  - Blackbelly Skatepark: Oct–Dec 2015, then Feb–Nov 2016
  - Piranha Pit: Feb–Dec 2016

**Splatoon 2 (Switch)**
- **At launch, returning stages were already bigger:**
  - Blackbelly's spawn platform was stretched backward and a side route was added. Inkipedia calls it "larger and more open than its appearance in the first game," going from the smallest stage in Splatoon to the fifth largest in Splatoon 2.
  - Moray Towers' center was expanded and its towers widened.
  - Port Mackerel's turf was widened and crates shrunk or made inkable.
- **Ver. 2.1.0 (Dec 2017):** Starfish Mainstage's center obstacles were cut and a slope added. Moray Towers was also changed.
- **Ver. 2.3.0 (Feb 2018), Rainmaker fixes:**
  - MakoMart Inkrails were moved so they "cannot be used to carry the Rainmaker to a high platform in your team's base."
  - Arowana Mall's Rainmaker-Free sign was moved.
  - Port Mackerel walls near spawn were fixed so players can swim up them.
- **Ver. 3.0.0 (Apr 2018):**
  - Fixed a Blackbelly Skatepark exploit that let Rainmaker players get "deep into enemy turf from above the goal." The same fix was made for Manta Maria.
  - Ranked was also narrowed to eight stages per mode, changing monthly. The Squid Research Lab said that with "over 20 stages… across 4 different battle types… it takes an extremely long time before a specific battle type and stage combination reoccurs. This may keep players from mastering their strategy." This let Nintendo quietly leave bad Rainmaker pairings out.
- **Blackbelly Skatepark's Rainmaker absence:** its Rainmaker layout didn't appear in any Ranked or League rotation from 16 Dec 2017 until the 4.3.0 rework. That is about a year, and the longest absence of any stage in both games.
- **The 2018 renovation wave**, each preceded by a "closed for renovations" notice with a scribbled-over blueprint:
  - **Starfish Mainstage, Ver. 4.0.0 (14 Sep 2018):** spawn area expanded, new terrain on each side, extra ramps in the Ranked modes except Tower Control, Inkrail right of spawn, and "The Rainmaker podium has been moved back significantly."
  - **The Reef, Ver. 4.2.0 (7 Nov 2018):**
    - Spawn areas "deepened and widened, which allow easier recovery from spawn camping" (Inkipedia's reading).
    - More routes from spawn to the flanks.
    - Surrounding buildings "moved back, making [the center] larger."
    - Rainmaker moved from below the bridge to on top of it.
    - Goal moved to the layer below spawn, with a ramp and an uninkable ramp as routes to the pedestal.
  - **Blackbelly Skatepark, Ver. 4.3.0 (5 Dec 2018):** spawn areas "significantly deepened and widened," obstacles moved and added. Inkipedia notes "one of the changes made was to adjust the balance in [Rainmaker]," after which Rainmaker returned. A 4.4.0 fix mentions "the slopes added for this mode," confirming Rainmaker-only terrain.
  - **Kelp Dome, Ver. 4.4.0 (Jan 2019):** "Changed the terrain on the left of the spawn point in Tower Control, Rainmaker and Clam Blitz."
- **Later fixes:** 4.x updates kept closing Rainmaker base-entry exploits, such as Inkrail routes into the enemy base on Piranha Pit and Arowana Mall. Version 4.9.0 fixed a Goby Arena crate glitch that launched players toward the enemy spawn in Rainmaker.

**Splatoon 3 (Switch)**
- **Returning stages were remade, not always bigger:**
  - Hammerhead Bridge became a finished bridge with its grates filled into solid concrete, "a narrow stage with fewer chasms."
  - Inkipedia says Mahi-Mahi Resort had "a completely different layout… more narrow and smaller" than in Splatoon.
  - This fed a widespread community complaint that launch stages were long, narrow "hallway" maps.
  - The Ranked mode was renamed Anarchy Battle, and checkpoints were added partly to make Rainmaker less of an instant win.
- **Mahi-Mahi Resort, Ver. 5.1.0 (18 Oct 2023):** spawn pushed back; the level below spawn extended; an Inkrail out of spawn; the center "significantly wider"; more ground when the water drops; an extra island; tall inkable columns. Inkipedia says the goal was "larger and more open (though still not to the degree of its Splatoon counterpart)." Nintendo's note just says "terrain… changed in all modes."
- **Mincemeat Metalworks, Ver. 6.1.0 (Jan 2024):** terrain changed in all modes.
- **Undertow Spillway, Ver. 7.2.0 (18 Apr 2024):** closed for "inspections and expansion work." Changes: left spawn extended, a perch ramp added, a tunnel under the snipe area, part of the central dividing wall removed. The Nintendo Life comment section showed player frustration beforehand ("so awkward to move around").
- **Ver. 8.0.0 (31 May 2024), the biggest wave:**
  - Hammerhead Bridge and Barnacle & Dime were changed "in all modes to increase routes into the center and options for strategic points." Hammerhead's perch area was widened, with new ramps, cover and extensions. This is the only stated purpose I found for any rework.
  - Museum d'Alfonsino got changes in all modes, including "the shape of walls surrounding the center" in specific modes. A community patch breakdown noted Museum gets "extra changes in Splat Zones and Rainmaker specifically."
  - Um'ami Ruins, Brinewater Springs and Scorch Gorge: terrain changed.
  - Eeltail Alley, Hagglefish Market, Flounder Heights, Marlin Airport and Manta Maria: "slightly changed."
  - Rainmaker fixes in the same patch:
    - The Rainmaker shield no longer shreds dualie rollers and splatana lunges that run into it.
    - Hagglefish Market: an exploit where a Squid Roll at a checkpoint got players into unintended areas was closed.
    - Inkblot Art Academy: "Don't retreat!" didn't show in places "difficult for opponent players to get into."
    - Undertow Spillway: "Don't retreat!" wrongly showed while advancing.
    - Humpback Pump Track: a lag-related distance exploit was fixed.
- **Other Splatoon 3 Rainmaker fixes:**
  - 2.1.0: Hagglefish Market and Museum d'Alfonsino scores not changing smoothly.
  - 3.0.0: an Undertow Spillway sponge exploit that changed goal distance a lot.
  - 3.1.0: Hammerhead Bridge narrow-space access.
  - 7.0.0: Triple Splashdown from the goal on Hammerhead Bridge letting players invade the enemy base.
  - Various: Rainmaker-Free Zone gaps on Eeltail Alley and Brinewater Springs.
- **Where the requested stages fit:**
  - Sturgeon Shipyard, Inkblot Art Academy, Wahoo World, Humpback Pump Track, Robo ROM-en, Manta Maria and Flounder Heights returned or appeared with their own Rainmaker layouts. Most have Rainmaker-Free Zones and later got fixes.
  - None besides those listed above had a documented major Rainmaker-driven rework.
  - Marlin Airport is a new Splatoon 3 stage (Fresh Season 2024), not a returning one.

### 4. Developer commentary: what exists and what doesn't
- **Nothing Rainmaker-specific from developers.** My research, plus a dedicated sub-search, found no primary or reliably translated quote in which Nogami, Sakaguchi, Amano, Sato or a stage designer explains designing stages for Rainmaker or why the 2018 or 2023–24 renovations happened. Official "why" statements are limited to patch-note phrases like "increase routes into the center and options for strategic points" (8.0.0).
- **The stage-pool philosophy is official.** Squid Research Lab, 2018: the rotation should let players master specific mode-and-stage pairings.
- **Famitsu, Dec 2015** (Nogami, Sakaguchi, Amano; English only as Nintendo Everything's summary):
  - Content stopped at 14 stages and 70+ weapons partly because of how hard "weapon compatibility with stages" becomes.
  - The new Piranha Pit and Ancho-V Games would be "very narrow," with "new, unusual play styles" expected.
- **Nintendo Dream, 2015** (Amano, via a fan forum rendering): only two stages rotate at a time so that stage-specific weapon choices make sense ("I'll go with a charger for these two maps").
- **Ask the Developer Vol. 7 (Splatoon 3, 2022):** only stage lore and setting, e.g. Sato on the Splatlands being "much more spacious, open," and Undertow Spillway being "renovated as a battle stage."
- **Unverified lead:** a fan-translated Famitsu interview (27 Apr 2023) reportedly asks the developers about the "long and narrow" stages. I have not verified its content.
- **Hiring ad, not a statement:** a February 2021 Nintendo job listing for a Splatoon 3 level designer mentioned renovating "existing" stages.

### 5. Competitive community view (opinion, not confirmed fact)
- **Old Blackbelly Skatepark (S1 and early S2)** is widely seen as the worst Rainmaker map. Nintendo's own action backs this up: it pulled the pairing for about a year and reworked the stage.
- **Rainmaker's early reputation** was a "quick mode that often ended before it really began, making it highly unbalanced" (TheGamer's Splatoon 3 guide). This fits with checkpoints arriving in Splatoon 3.
- **TV Tropes' fan notes** say Splatoon 2 Blackbelly's open layout put the goal close to the start "before an update expanded this map-mode's size." They describe Splatoon 1 Port Mackerel as tarp-covered chokepoints where strong teams could trap opponents in spawn.
- **Squidboards map-pool discussions (Splatoon 3):**
  - One sample Low Ink Rainmaker pool used Scorch Gorge, Undertow Spillway, Humpback Pump Track, Crableg Capital and Manta Maria. That suggests stages with open, multi-lane approaches are preferred.
  - Players note that stage/mode suitability changes between games; for example, the Sting Ray special made Splatoon 2 Rainmaker "frustrating."
- **Community feedback and renovations:** it's plausible the "narrow hallway" complaints about Splatoon 3 fed into the 2023–24 reworks, since every one of them widened space or added routes. Nintendo hasn't said so.

## Recommendations (for reading the evidence or building map pools)
1. Treat the patch notes (Nintendo Support update histories) as the authority on *what* changed and when. Treat Inkipedia's change lists as detailed but secondary. Treat any stated *reason* ("for Rainmaker," "anti-spawn-camp") as a reading of Nintendo's changes unless it's in Nintendo's own words.
2. For Rainmaker pools, favor stages with at least two workable routes to each checkpoint, deep spawns, and Rainmaker-Free Zones that cover unreachable high ground. Be wary of stages or versions where the goal is close to the center. The reworked versions (post-4.3.0 Blackbelly, post-5.1.0 Mahi-Mahi, post-8.0.0 Hammerhead) are the better-tested ones.
3. To close the developer-commentary gap, the next sources to check are the original Japanese Famitsu and Nintendo Dream issues (Dec 2015, Splatoon 2's 2017 launch and 2018 anniversary, April 2023) and the SplatoonJP renovation tweets.
